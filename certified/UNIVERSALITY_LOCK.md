> §9 (universal $\{M_n, O_n\}$ dichotomy) into three tier-disjoint

## §9.1 (i) — THEOREM block, $n \in \{7, 9, 11\}$

> **Theorem F (Stabilizer / lattice / orbit-distance dichotomy at
> $n \in \{7, 9, 11\}$).** *Let $n \in \{7, 9, 11\}$, and let
> $M_n,\ O_n \in V_{\mathrm{std},n}$ denote the canonical sparse-low
> templates. Acting by the signed coordinate-permutation group
> $G^*_n = S_n \times \{\pm 1\}$ on $A_{n-1}$:*
>
> 1. *the stabilizer of $M_n$ is*
>    $$\mathrm{Stab}_{G^*}(M_n) \;\cong\; D_4 \times S_{n-4},$$
>    *and the stabilizer of $O_n$ is*
>    $$\mathrm{Stab}_{G^*}(O_n) \;\cong\; C_2 \times S_{n-4};$$
> 2. *the integer lattices generated by the $G^*$-orbits saturate the
>    root lattice $A_{n-1}$, i.e.*
>    $$\Lambda_{M_n} \;=\; \Lambda_{O_n} \;=\; V_{\mathrm{std},n} \cap \mathbb{Z}^n \;=\; A_{n-1};$$
> 3. *the orbit of $O_n$ has graph-distance $2$ from $M_n$ in the
>    Cayley graph of $G^*$ acting on $V_{\mathrm{std},n}$:*
>    $$d_{M_n}\bigl(\mathrm{orb}_{G^*}(O_n)\bigr) \;=\; 2.$$
>
> *The proof is by direct identification of the abstract groups
> (1) and explicit Smith-normal-form computation of the orbit
> lattices (2); (3) is a finite check on the Cayley graph at each
> $n$.*
>

## §9.2 (ii) — PROPOSITION (exhaustive) block, $5 \le n \le 13$

> **Proposition P4 (Exhaustive verification of the dichotomy bridges
> for $5 \le n \le 13$).** *For every integer $n$ with*
> $5 \le n \le 13$, *the predicted stabilizer sizes,
> orbit sizes, lattice ranks, elementary-divisor patterns, and
> orbit-distances of the canonical templates $M_n,\ O_n$ — as
> tabulated in §2.4 — are reproduced **exactly** by exhaustive
> enumeration of the relevant $G^*$-action.*
>
> *Concretely, the verification consists of nine independent finite
> checks (one per $n \in \{5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13\}$): each
> confirms*
>
> - $|\mathrm{Stab}_{G^*}(M_n)| = 8 \cdot (n-4)!$ *and*
>   $|\mathrm{Stab}_{G^*}(O_n)| = 2 \cdot (n-4)!$;
> - *the kernel-order decomposition of each stabilizer matches the
>   $D_4 \times S_{n-4}$ / $C_2 \times S_{n-4}$ semidirect split;*
> - $\Lambda_{M_n}$ *and* $\Lambda_{O_n}$ *both have rank $n-1$ and
>   all elementary divisors equal to $1$;*
> - $d_{M_n}(\mathrm{orb}_{G^*}(O_n)) = 2$.
>
> *No analytic argument is used; the proposition is the literal
> output of the enumeration.*
>
> **Tier.** EXHAUSTIVE. *Source.*
> `PHASE_9_13_S9_REPORT.md`
> §8.27 (γ-1) (historical source-workspace provenance label, not
> bundled here). *Certificate.*
> [`stabilizers_5_to_13.json`](stabilizers_5_to_13.json).
>
> **Scope caveat.** Proposition P4 is a *computer verification* over a
> finite range of $n$. It does **not** prove the dichotomy for any
> $n \ge 14$, nor does it imply Theorem F for $n \in \{5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13\}$
> (the tighter analytic identifications of Theorem F at those values
> are not in scope of P4 and are not claimed elsewhere).

## §9.3 (iii) — CONJECTURE block, all $n \ge 5$

> **Conjecture C10 (Universal $\{M_n, O_n\}$ dichotomy).** *For
> every integer $n \ge 5$, the canonical sparse-low templates
> $M_n,\ O_n \in V_{\mathrm{std},n}$ — defined as in §2.4 by the
> rule extending the $n \in \{7, 9, 11\}$ cases — satisfy:*
>
> 1. $\mathrm{Stab}_{G^*}(M_n) \cong D_4 \times S_{n-4}$
>    *and*
>    $\mathrm{Stab}_{G^*}(O_n) \cong C_2 \times S_{n-4}$;
> 2. $\Lambda_{M_n} = \Lambda_{O_n} = V_{\mathrm{std},n} \cap \mathbb{Z}^n = A_{n-1}$;
> 3. $d_{M_n}\bigl(\mathrm{orb}_{G^*}(O_n)\bigr) = 2$.
>
> **Evidence.** The conjecture is supported by exactly two pieces of
> evidence, and no other:
>
> - **Theorem F** (analytic identification at $n \in \{7, 9, 11\}$);
> - **Proposition P4** (exhaustive computational verification of the
>   bridges for every $n$ in $\{5, 6, \ldots, 13\}$).
>
> No further analytic, asymptotic, or large-$n$ heuristic argument
> is invoked.
>
> **Tier.** CONJECTURE. *Status.* Open for $n \ge 14$ in the analytic
> form (1) (although stabilizer sizes are forced by the $S_{n-4}$
> factor for $n \le 13$, the abstract-group identification is open
> beyond $n = 11$); open for all $n \ge 14$ in the exhaustive form;
> the lattice-saturation clause (2) and the orbit-distance clause
> (3) are open in full generality.
